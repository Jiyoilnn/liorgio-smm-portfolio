How I made it

AI does the first draft. I direct it, check it and finish it.

  1. Step 1

    Brief to prompt

    I turned the brief into a shot-by-shot prompt for a 15-second, 9:16 ad: fast cuts, bright sunny light, and FOOLY’s turquoise, lemon, mango and peach palette.

  2. Step 2

    Generate in Google Flow

    I generated the scenes in Google Flow (Omni 1.2 Flash), with FOOLY’s product photos as reference images so the can matches the brand.

  3. Step 3

    Edit and caption

    I edited the clips, added captions in FOOLY’s colors, and exported a captioned version and a clean version without text.

Strategy & copy

Spec work: the research, angles, ad scripts and advertorial I would write for FOOLY. Not commissioned by FOOLY, and every product claim comes only from the brand's own brief.

Who it's for

Maya, 34, a desk worker who does fine in the morning but loses to cravings at 3–4 PM and 9–11 PM. She has tried snacks, fiber drinks and gummies, and worries about bloating, bad taste and sugar.

Awareness and sophistication

Most buyers are problem-aware: they know they snack when they aren't hungry. The appetite market has heard “feel full” many times, so the ads win with identification (the exact craving moment) and a mechanism (full without the bloat, nothing to mix).

Two angles to test

1. The 3 PM moment for problem-aware buyers.
2. “I tried everything else” for buyers already let down by other products. Same budget, judged on cost per purchase.

Sponsored

Why you snack at 3 PM when you're not even hungry, and the 25-calorie habit that helps

You eat a good lunch. You're not hungry. And yet by 3 PM, you're standing at the pantry or the vending machine, looking for something.

If that sounds familiar, you're not lacking willpower. Afternoon snacking is often about boredom, tiredness or habit, not hunger. That's why “just drink water” and “just say no” rarely last more than a few days.

The usual fixes come with a catch. Low-calorie snacks add up fast. Fiber powders can leave you bloated. And many people say appetite gummies don't do much at all.

A different kind of afternoon drink. FOOLY is a functional tea made to help you feel full, fast. Open a cold can, drink it, and in about 5 to 10 minutes the snacky feeling settles. Each can has 25 calories, no sugar and no artificial flavors, and it's made to fill you up without that heavy, bloated feeling.

  • It's easy. No mixing, no chewing, no measuring. Keep one in your bag or desk.
  • It tastes good. Lemon, Mango and Peach, like a real iced tea.
  • It fits your goals. Managing your weight or need light fuel before a workout: 25 calories, no sugar.

How to use it. Reach for a FOOLY when the craving hits, most often mid-afternoon or after dinner. Give it 5 to 10 minutes, then notice how much easier it is to skip the snack.

Try FOOLY for yourself at DrinkFooly.com.

Testing plan

  1. Angle: Script 1 vs. Script 2, same budget and audience, judged on cost per purchase.
  2. Hooks: the winner's three hooks, judged on hook rate and click-through rate.
  3. Format and page: advertorial vs. product page, and the winning hook as a static and a carousel.
